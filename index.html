I am a third-year Physics undergraduate at the National University of Singapore (NUS) and an exchange student at Cornell University for Fall 2026. My academic passion lies at the intersection of quantum information and condensed matter physics. I am particularly driven by the challenge of understanding complex quantum phenomena and translating foundational physical theories into scalable quantum technologies.

Currently at Cornell University, I conduct research on open quantum systems and non-equilibrium dynamics in the driven-dissipative Bose-Hubbard model under Prof. Erich Mueller. Previously at the Centre for Quantum Technologies (CQT), I developed optical breadboard systems and laser frequency stabilization for trapped ytterbium ion ($Yb^+$) processors under Prof. Dzmitry Matsukevich.

Supervisor: Prof. Erich Mueller

Steady States & Non-Equilibrium Dynamics in the Driven-Dissipative Bose-Hubbard Model

  • Core Objective: To identify and characterize steady states and spontaneous symmetry breaking within the driven-dissipative Bose-Hubbard model.
  • Physics Focus: Analyzing the complex out-of-equilibrium dynamics caused by the competition between coherent external driving, on-site interactions, and environmental dissipation.
  • Methods: Developing simulation codes using tensor network algorithms (via ITensors in Julia) to solve the open quantum system dynamics and explore non-equilibrium phase transitions.
Open Quantum Systems Driven-Dissipative Systems Bose-Hubbard Model Tensor Networks ITensors.jl Julia Phase Transitions

Jan 2026 – Jun 2026

Supervisor: Prof. Dzmitry Matsukevich

Modular Optical Breadboard & Laser Frequency Stabilization for Trapped-Ion ($Yb^+$) Quantum Computing

  • Designed a compact ($25 \times 50\text{ cm}$) and modular optical breadboard for stabilising a $369\text{ nm}$ laser used in trapped ytterbium ion ($Yb^+$) quantum computing processors.
  • Optimized optical layout and component placements using PyOpticL to minimize physical footprint while maintaining optimal beam propagation and cavity coupling efficiency.
  • Aligned optical components on a vibration-isolated optical table; processed optical cavity error signals and implemented feedback control algorithms for robust laser locking.
Quantum Computing Trapped Ions ($Yb^+$) Laser Locking PyOpticL AMO Optics
